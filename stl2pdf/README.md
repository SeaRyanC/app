## STL2PDF – STL Cross-Section Viewer

STL2PDF lets you load any `.stl` file and interactively explore cross-sections through it, then print those sections to an accurately dimensioned PDF. The app runs entirely in your browser — no file is ever uploaded to a server. No STL handy? Click one of the built-in demo models (Torus, Stepped Shaft, Mounting Plate) to try the app instantly.

Open an STL file by dragging it onto the page, clicking to browse, pasting, or choosing a demo model — a brief loading spinner is shown while large files parse. The model is shown in three orthographic views — Front (XZ), Side (YZ), and Top (XY) — with Z pointing up, all sharing the same initial zoom level so proportions are easy to compare between views. Click anywhere in a Front or Side view to place a cutting plane, which instantly renders the 2D cross-section in the Section Output pane. The indicator line can be dragged up or down to move the plane, and right-clicking it cycles the plane orientation through horizontal (Z), vertical-X, and vertical-Y. Every pane supports zoom (scroll wheel) and pan (right-click drag).

In the Section Output pane, the cross-section is drawn engineering-drawing style: interior material shaded light gray, empty space white, and a thick black outline. A toolbar above the section offers three snapping measurement tools: **📏 Ruler** for point-to-point distances, **⊙ Diameter** for fitting a circle to a hole or boss, and **⬛ Auto-Dim** for one-click width/height dimensioning of a region — only one tool is active at a time, and right-click clears the current measurement. When you're happy with the slice, click **Print PDF** to download a Letter-size PDF of the cross-section at true 1 : 1 millimetre scale. Sections that don't fit on one page are never scaled down — they're tiled across multiple 1 : 1 pages instead, each labelled and carrying a 25.4 mm calibration bar, so printed tiles can be measured directly or taped together at true size.
